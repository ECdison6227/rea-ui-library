Using Hark Pro

Hark is the operating system for your life, built to think ahead, and help you with real, practical things. It works for your private and professional life, for simple errands and serious projects.

All handled in a single, persistent chat thread.
No juggling “agents,” “tokens,” or other technical oddities required.

Hark can do everything you’ve come to expect from AI. But instead of optimizing for code, Hark is focused more on helping with your everyday items.

By combining advanced memory and the ability to use a computer in the cloud, Hark understands the context of your life and can do things for you across the web.

Use Hark to order groceries. Manage your calendar. Build mini apps. Book flights. Summarize Slack threads. And track complicated projects while integrating Google Docs.

You just ask, and Hark will work to make it happen.

Features

Hark is designed to think with you and sometimes ahead of you. But mostly, it’s designed to save you time through a series of intentional features that drive action rather than just talk.

Imagine computers that use themselves, and personalized buttons that will pay your bill or book a ticket with a tap. All accessible through natural language. This is Hark.

Hark will always ask the least of you possible to do the most for you that it can. It’s built to help.

Hark has three main views: Home, Chat, and Projects. Home holds Action Cards and Panels for you. Chat is your main thread on Hark, where Handoffs happen. And Projects are basically just Chat, but organized into individual topical threads if you prefer to keep projects contained.

Handoff is a computer in the cloud, operated by Hark. It can sign into websites for you and take actions (like buying things and filling out web forms) on your behalf.

To start a Handoff, all you do is ask Hark to do something that requires it to use a web browser. Hark will take it from there.

The longer you use Hark, the better it will know you, and the more naturally it can help. This is because Hark learns from your conversations, and it applies that context to everything you ask it.

You can. Just ask Hark to forget a memory and it will.

Panels are tiny bits of software built by Hark - ranging from exercise trackers to media players - that live right inside the app within Home. All you do is ask Hark to build one, and it will construct itself right inside Home.

You can even tap “Reply” on your Panels to redesign them or dig deeper into their data, unlocking even more from services you already use like Strava and Venmo. Talk to your panels just like you talk to Hark.

Notifications give you problems. Action Buttons give you solutions. Generated by Hark, each card offers you an action with a button press: “Pay” a bill, or “Cancel” a subscription. These cards are generated from sources like what Hark sees in your email and has learned from your conversations.

Hark can manage your life in one, long-running chat. But sometimes you want to start with a blank slate. Projects are dedicated threads for longer term work. They focus a single topic, and related files, in one place that you can visit again and again over time.

Specialties are workflows that Hark memorizes to repeat a multi-step task you reliably over and over again. Imagine there’s an exact way you want product returns to be negotiated, or a complex workflow where you set up data analyses from multiple specific sources.

Hark sets up these specialties automatically for you, or you can ask to create one.

Maybe you want to have a coffee delivered every day. Or set a reminder for a weekly meeting. These become Scheduled Tasks. You can create them just by asking Hark.

Hark can use thousands of sites through Handoff on the web. But it also supports direct API connections we call Services. Current supported Services include Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and more.

The background you see in Hark is a realtime simulation of the sky, wherever you are on earth. It takes into account your location, windspeed, cloud cover, particulates, and time of day. So the sun will rise and set on your screen just as it does in the atmosphere, every day.

Hark can have a few minute delay when tracking things like food deliveries, so it’s possible external push notifications will arrive shortly before Hark’s.

Security and Privacy

In short: We recognize that your data belongs to you. We limit and audit employee access to accounts. And we’ll never sell your Hark data or share it with advertisers. But our full privacy policy is here.

Your most sensitive data—ranging from a credit card to a website login—is stored in an encrypted vault we call Secured by Hark. It’s also encrypted in transit. That means Hark cannot view this information ever. Instead, it’s only detokenized on the actual page (like a retail site) where it’s needed.

Safeguards and Our Point of View on AI

We’re building Hark to help us escape screens and spend more time with friends and loved ones. AI is a tool that can improve our lives. It’s not a replacement for humanity.

Hark is designed to avoid parasocial behavior. It is not built to be a bestie, romantic partner, or a psychologist. Instead, Hark is optimized to help you out in general and encompassing ways. In moments of extreme need, it is designed to encourage you to connect with people.

Hark is not in the business of censorship. It shouldn’t assist with direct, harmful actions, but it generally won’t limit access to publicly available information, either. If something is available in major search engines, it probably will be on Hark, too. For research, Hark operates with academic detachment. While it may share more about a deplorable topic, that does not mean Hark endorses said topic.
